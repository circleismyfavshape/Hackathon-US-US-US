# Research and references

These sources explain the problem, support the contact details and credit the project's underlying data. They do not validate the simulated flood boundaries or imply endorsement of FLARE.

## The problem

- [Department of Statistics Malaysia: Special Report on Impact of Floods in Malaysia 2021](https://www.dosm.gov.my/index.php/portal-main/release-content/special-report-on-impact-of-floods-in-malaysia-2021), released 28 January 2022. DOSM recorded RM6.1 billion in losses from the late-2021 and early-2022 floods, covering homes, vehicles, businesses and infrastructure. This is historical context, not a current incident report.
- [World Bank: Flood Risk Management — Leveraging Finance for Business Resilience in Malaysia](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/malaysia/publication/flood-risk-management-leveraging-finance-for-business-resilience-in-malaysia). Provides context on flood preparedness and resilience. FLARE focuses on the narrower interface problem of turning location and incident information into clear next steps.

## Public information

- [Malaysia government: emergency procedures during a disaster](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/topics/emergency-procedures-during-a-disaster).
- [Civil Defence: emergency service 999](https://www.civildefence.gov.my/perkhidmatan-kecemasan-999/). Confirms the integrated emergency number; 991 was merged into 999 and is not presented as a separate current emergency line in FLARE.
- [NADMA situation report, 16 October 2025](https://mydims.nadma.gov.my/storage/Laporan%20Situasi%20Semasa/2025/Laporan%20Situasi%20Semasa%2016-10-2025.pdf), lists the NDCC hotline `03-8064 2400`.
- [MET Malaysia weather API documentation](https://developer.data.gov.my/realtime-api/weather). Source of live regional warnings, with attribution to MET Malaysia and CC BY 4.0 terms.
